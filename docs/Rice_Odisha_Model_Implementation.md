# Odisha model execution rules

Declared before fitting or opening newer outcomes. The [original protocol](Rice_Odisha_Seasonal_Protocol.md) and [timing amendment](Rice_Odisha_Timing_Amendment.md) remain unchanged.

There are 13 declared configurations. Both tree algorithms use fixed one-hot district categories derived from the 30-district study inventory, without target encoding. All models learn deviations from the five-yield historical reference; their final forecasts are clipped at zero. Ridge standardization uses training rows only. Histogram boosting runs its declared 150 iterations without an internal random validation split. Parallel numeric work is bounded to two threads. Baseline trend extrapolation advances four years from the centre of the Y−6…Y−2 history to Y.

Before reading assessment cells, save candidate order, development scores, selected configuration, interval calibration, exact source/preparation/code hashes and library versions. Also record the best history Ridge and best weather Ridge from development only. Assessment reports all 13 fixed configurations; its primary result always belongs to the development-selected configuration. Showing the others does not authorize selecting a new winner on assessment. A paired Ridge comparison tests the weather block with the same model family; comparing a weather tree against a history Ridge also changes algorithm and cannot isolate weather's contribution. No additional history-only tree configurations are introduced.

Numeric label-quality rules, fixed before fitting: use source yield in tonnes/ha multiplied by 1,000. Require finite area > 0, production >= 0 and yield >= 0. Accept a zero yield when those checks and source rounding consistency pass; zero is not automatically a missing value or discarded crop failure. Require the source yield to agree with 1,000 × production / area within **5.01 kg/ha**, allowing rounding to 0.01 tonnes/ha and floating-point tolerance. Record all exclusions and their reasons. These are measurement checks, not outlier trimming based on model errors. A label that fails them cannot become a lag predictor. All candidates use identical eligible records with complete history and weather. Target-year area and production remain audit-only.

Calibration uses the selected model's 90 rolling predictions for 2014–2016, with training through Y−2. The finite-sample quantile rank is ceil((n+1) × 0.8), capped at n, on absolute error divided by the positive historical reference. Record the scale before accessing assessment labels. Bounds clip at zero; the interval width therefore need not equal twice the nominal half-width. Calibration coverage is not an independent test of uncertainty.

Assessment is a sequential historical replay of 2018–2022. The fixed annual-refitting rule permits earlier assessment labels to enter later fitting when at least two years old. It does not permit later labels to enter an earlier forecast. Point-forecast low-yield flags use the frozen 80%-of-history threshold; no risk probability is invented. Undefined precision/recall or constant-target R squared is reported as unavailable. Bootstrap intervals resample whole years, with the declared 2,000 repetitions and seed 42. Few assessment years and spatial dependence limit what those intervals can establish.

The five-state annual estimator, prior experiments and original preparation outputs are preserved. New results are a separate study using fixed 2011 reference geography and revised weather archives, not certified annual boundaries, real-time vintages, protein measurements or causal damage estimates.
